Strategic Investment Roadmap 2026–2035

The Emerging Bangsamoro

Integrated Ecosystem Development Strategy

A comprehensive roadmap transforming vision into measurable economic transformation through synchronized cluster development, strategic investment pathways, and inclusive growth anchored in moral governance

Open the Platform → Read the Report
Headline indicators: the roadmap tracks BARMM's economic transformation from a ₱266.59B GRDP baseline (2025, five provinces) to ₱550B+ target by 2035, with integrated metrics across investment approvals, export growth, poverty reduction, and infrastructure development — aligned with the six Pareto headline KPIs and the Chapter 5 Balanced Scorecard causal architecture (Learning & Growth 20% → Internal Process 25% → Stakeholder 25% → Financial 30%).

Vision 2035: A Hub for Resilient and Ethical Growth

By 2035, the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region will be recognized as Southeast Asia's premier halal, agro-industrial, and sustainable investment destination—a self-sustaining, export-linked economy delivering inclusive prosperity anchored in Islamic values, cultural identity, and moral governance.

Vision framework: the vision rests on the Bangsamoro Economic and Investment Ecosystem (BEIE): four cross-cutting operating systems (moral governance, resilience, inclusivity and peace) anchoring five functional clusters (Foundations, Transformers, Enablers, Connectors and Financiers). The Integrated Ecosystem Development Strategy synchronises the clusters around halal and agro-industrial value chains, the green economy, enabling infrastructure, BIMP-EAGA markets and Islamic finance. Target outcomes: ₱550B+ GRDP, at least 20,000 jobs and poverty incidence below 20% by 2035.

Balanced Scorecard Strategic Perspectives

Chapter 5 translates the IEDS into 27 strategic objectives across four perspectives, with causal weights Learning & Growth (20%) → Internal Process (25%) → Stakeholder (25%) → Financial (30%). The 18 objectives with headline targets are shown below, each with its baseline, 2030 checkpoint, 2035 target and Critical Leverage Point. All 27 in Chapter 5.

F1: Increase Investment Approvals

Target: ₱15B+ annual investment approvals by 2035

₱5.0B approved in 2025, and ₱5.1B already in January–April 2026. Checkpoint: ₱10B a year by 2030. Levers: investment promotion roadshows, BIMP-EAGA missions and investor aftercare. LP1 · LP3

F2: Grow Regional Economy

Target: ₱550B+ GRDP by 2035

From ₱266.59B in 2025 (five provinces, constant 2018 prices), through ₱331B at the 2028 gate and ₱383B at the 2030 checkpoint. Levers: BEIE ecosystem synchronisation, PPP frameworks and value-chain development. LP1 · LP2

F3: Expand Export Revenue

Target: ₱40B+ annual exports by 2035

From under ₱10B (draft baseline, to be verified with the data owner); ₱26B by 2030. Levers: UAE halal corridor, BIMP-EAGA trade facilitation and export-readiness programmes. LP1

F4: Activate Green Economy Revenue

Target: ₱500M a year in carbon and PES revenue by 2035

From zero; ₱100M a year by 2030. Levers: REDD+ registration, JMC No. 2026-01 implementation and carbon-market access, gated by the Bangsamoro Forestry Code. LP5

F5: Mobilize Islamic Finance

Target: ₱20B+ Islamic banking assets by 2035

From under ₱2B; ₱8B by 2030. Levers: Al-Amanah expansion, Islamic finance literacy and fintech integration. LP4

S1: Enhance Investor Satisfaction

Target: 8.0+/10 investor satisfaction by 2035

Not yet measured; 7.0/10 by 2030. Levers: aftercare, an investor ombudsman and regulatory stability. S2 targets 85% project retention. LP3

S3: Empower MSMEs through Certification

Target: 5,000+ halal-certified MSMEs by 2035

From about 500 (draft baseline, to be verified); 3,000 by 2030. Levers: the BHB MSME certification programme, BHIDP implementation and capacity building. LP1

S4: Reduce Poverty

Target: Poverty incidence below 20% by 2035

23.5% of families (PSA, full-year 2023). The 2030 milestone of below 25% is already met and is being recalibrated. Levers: jobs-focused investment facilitation, MSME development and inclusive value chains. LP1 · LP2

S5: Improve Community Access to Finance

Target: 70%+ adult financial inclusion by 2035

About 38% today, pending a harmonised definition; 55% by 2030. Levers: Islamic microfinance, fintech, CARD expansion and waqf activation. LP4

S6: Create Quality Employment

Target: 20,000+ jobs by 2035

2,029 jobs reported by investment-approved firms in 2025; 10,000+ by 2030. Levers: labour-intensive sector priorities, skills matching and TESDA alignment. LP1 · LP3

P1: Streamline Investment Facilitation

Target: 1-day digital business registration by 2035

From several days; 2 days by 2030. Levers: the digital BNR system, BEGMP e-governance and one-stop-shop expansion. LP3

P3: Strengthen Halal Certification

Target: Full OIC/SMIIC accreditation and an MRA by 2035

Accreditation pending; provisional alignment by 2030. Certification time falls from 45–60 days to 30 days by 2030 and 21 days or less by 2035. Levers: BHB capacity, international MRAs and QA laboratories. LP1

P4: Accelerate Infrastructure Delivery

Target: Over 90% infrastructure budget execution by 2035

From under 80%; 88% by 2030. Levers: MPW–BOI–LGU coordination, right-of-way resolution and PPP frameworks. LP2

P5: Synchronize BMOA Programs

Target: 8/10 inter-agency coordination score by 2035

From about 5.5/10; 7.5/10 by 2030. Levers: one coordinated operating model (BIF-Net and its Secretariat, subject to legal review), joint M&E and inter-agency MOAs. LP3

L1: Build Halal Expertise

Target: 100+ trained halal certification officers by 2035

From fewer than 50; 70 by 2030. Levers: a Halal Training Academy, the BHB–SMIIC partnership and international secondments. LP1

L2: Develop Islamic Finance Professionals

Target: 50+ Islamic finance professionals by 2035

From fewer than 20; 35 by 2030. Levers: BSP-aligned scholarships, professional certification and IsDB training. LP4

L3: Strengthen IPA Capacity

Target: 80% of investment promotion staff certified or trained by 2035

From under 50%; 70% by 2030. Levers: capacity-building programmes, the BOI capacity-development plan and performance incentives. LP3

L5: Improve Functional Literacy

Target: 75%+ functional literacy by 2035

From 64.7% (PSA 2024 FLEMMS, ages 10–64); 72% by 2030. Levers: TESDA programmes, DepEd alternative learning and adult literacy campaigns. LP1

Four Strategic Options Analysis

Comprehensive evaluation of alternative development pathways through multi-criteria assessment examining economic impact, feasibility, identity alignment, systems leverage, risk-return profile, inclusivity, and sustainability

Strategic Options Formulation: Through rigorous TOWS matrix analysis and systems archetype mapping, four distinct pathways emerged: (1) Halal Economy Dominance Strategy (HEDS) scoring 7.53/10, (2) Green Economy Monetization Strategy (GEMS) at 7.16/10, (3) Infrastructure-First Enabling Strategy (IFES) at 7.46/10, and (4) Integrated Ecosystem Development Strategy (IEDS) achieving the highest composite score of 8.83/10 under the canonical weights (Table 3-8), including the top systems-leverage score (10.0/10). IEDS ranks first in every weighting scenario tested, even a stress test that penalises its coordination burden (Table 3-10). Survey respondents ranked the options in the same order (IEDS 6.05, HEDS 5.90, IFES 5.77, GEMS 5.73; n = 75).

Phased Implementation Roadmap 2026–2035

Three-phase execution strategy (Chapter 6) with budget-calibrated interventions sequenced to match infrastructure readiness, institutional capacity, and market development. Indicative public catalytic envelope: ₱120–160 billion, a planning parameter rather than an appropriation; with private capital it supports a total investment requirement of about ₱0.99–1.13 trillion (roughly 6–9 times the public envelope). Phase 1 deploys 10 priority actions across all 5 Critical Leverage Points (CLPs).

01

Phase 1: Foundation Building

2026–2028 | Budget: ₱35–45 Billion

Establishing the moral governance operating system and deploying foundational enablers that unlock downstream investment viability. This phase prioritizes coherent sequencing logic by ensuring regulatory architecture, digital facilitation, and critical infrastructure are in place before scaling production or export commitments. Aligns with the Chapter 5 Phase 1 priority actions and the decisions for the first 180 days (Chapter 7, Table 7.11).

Key Deliverables:
  • BHB operationalized with OIC/SMIIC-aligned processes & digital traceability pilot
  • ZBIP construction milestones reached; one household-electrification metric adopted for all BIRD reporting (Chapter 7, A6)
  • Bangsamoro Forestry Code enacted; JMC No. 2026-01 signed & LGU capacity building initiated
  • 1-day digital business registration launched via BEGMP platform
  • Farm-to-market road priorities funded & ROW mechanisms streamlined
  • One coordinated operating model in place: a policy council, the BIF-Net network and its Secretariat in MTIT–BOI, subject to legal review (Chapter 7, A2)
02

Phase 2: Acceleration

2029–2031 | Budget: ₱50–65 Billion

Leveraging governance and infrastructure foundations to activate Foundations–Transformers Integration and Financiers–Ecosystem Alignment. With certification systems, energy connectivity, and logistics networks operational, focus shifts to scaling value addition and channeling capital across the ecosystem.

Key Deliverables:
  • Bangsamoro Halal Park operational (Matanog); BHB achieves international recognition
  • REDD+ program registered; first carbon credit & PES sales executed
  • Islamic banking branches in all provincial capitals; takaful & green sukuk launched
  • 3,000 MSMEs halal-certified and 2M annual tourist arrivals by 2030; Lake Lanao eco-tourism open
  • Cold chain & warehousing hubs operational in priority provinces
  • ₱100M/year carbon/PES revenue activated
03

Phase 3: Consolidation & Global Integration

2032–2035 | Budget: ₱35–50 Billion

Consolidating BARMM's position as a self-sustaining, export-linked, and inclusive economic hub. All strategic pillars converge to achieve Foundations–Transformers–Connectors maturity and global market recognition with innovation-driven continuous improvement.

Key Deliverables:
  • OIC endorsement secured; SMIIC Mutual Recognition Agreement (MRA) signed
  • ₱500M a year in carbon/PES revenue; 3M annual tourist arrivals
  • GRDP ₱550B+; poverty <20%; ₱15B+ annual investment approvals sustained
  • BARMM recognized as ASEAN halal hub; cross-border BIMP-EAGA certification harmonized
  • Innovation hubs & halal R&D centers operational; green bonds market mature
  • 90% household electrification (100% by 2040) & 85% broadband connectivity achieved

Integrated Roadmap Overview

Comprehensive visual synthesis of the Bangsamoro Investment Roadmap Development 2026–2035 showing strategic architecture, cluster interdependencies, and transformation pathways consistent with Chapters 5 and 6

Roadmap Infographic: This comprehensive visualization illustrates the synchronized ecosystem development approach, showing how the Cross-Cutting Operating Systems (Moral Governance, Resilience, Inclusivity, Peace) anchor the five functional clusters (Foundations, Transformers, Enablers, Connectors, Financiers) through three implementation phases, ultimately achieving the vision of a ₱550B+ GRDP economy with <20% poverty incidence by 2035. Indicative public catalytic envelope: ₱120–160 billion.

Shape the Future of Bangsamoro

The evidence points to a real peace dividend, rising investment approvals (₱5.0B in 2025, ₱5.1B in the first four months of 2026) and a regulatory architecture that is maturing. Trends do not deliver themselves; execution does. Investors, development partners and institutions can help turn scattered progress into synchronised ecosystem development.
