# Tawi-Tawi BIRD Validation Workshop — Facilitator Guide

Oct 27, 2026 · @Alvin

## At a glance

The workshop tests whether Tawi-Tawi stakeholders confirm, amend or reject the propositions BIRD 2026–2035 drafted for the province from secondary data. It does not generate new strategy. Results feed the first recalibration of the roadmap; they are not an endorsement.

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Date | 28 October 2026, 5 hours (suggested 08:00–13:30 with breaks) |
| Venue | Rachel's Place hotel, Bongao, Tawi-Tawi |
| Convenor | MTIT–Bureau of Investments (BoI) |
| Participants | 20–30 from the private sector and BMOAs |
| Output status | Input to recalibration only |
| Language | English slides; facilitation in Sinama, Tausug and Filipino as needed |
| Recording | Mentimeter (`mentimeter_pulse`, no PII) plus paper scoring sheets |

**Decision rules** (announce at the opening, apply in the synthesis):

| Share of participants who agree (score 4 or 5) | Result | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 70% or more | Validated | Kept as drafted |
| 50–69% | Validated with amendment | Kept, with the amendments recorded on the day |
| Below 50% | Contested | Carried to recalibration with the reasons given |

With 20–30 people, one participant moves a result by 3–5 percentage points. Report results as validation signals, not population estimates, and report counts beside percentages.

**Gaps this workshop closes.** The BIRD Validation Survey (n=76) had no respondents from Tawi-Tawi, yet 61.1% of respondents named Tawi-Tawi as the province with the most trapped value. Phase 3 (2032–2035, ₱35–50 billion indicative) and sequencing option C have no island input at all.

## Session scripts

Each round follows the same rhythm: read, score silently, discuss at tables, record amendments, report back.

**Round 1: Diagnosis check (75 minutes)**

1. Show the pre-filled Tawi-Tawi SWOT: 4–5 items per quadrant, drawn from the outlook and Chapter 3 (seaweed dominance; raw exports; cold chain and market-linkage gaps; BIMP-EAGA proximity; heat stress; island power cost). Add P5.
2. Silent scoring, 10 minutes: each participant marks every item Agree, Amend or Reject on Sheet A and in Mentimeter.
3. Table discussion, 35 minutes: tables take the three items with the most Amend or Reject marks and write the amended wording.
4. Missing items, 10 minutes: each table may add up to two items the SWOT lacks, with evidence.
5. Report back, 15 minutes: one minute per table, amendments only.

Facilitator prompt: "Is this true for your municipality, and what is your evidence?"

**Round 2: Strategy and sequencing check (75 minutes)**

1. Read P1–P4 and P6–P8 one at a time. Participants score each 1–5 on Sheet B before discussion.
2. For each proposition, tables answer two questions: what is the binding constraint, and which institution or firm must act first.
3. P7: show the four options with one line each; participants rank them independently.
4. P8: show the three sequences side by side; participants pick one and write one reason.

Facilitator prompt: "What would have to be true for this to work in Tawi-Tawi?"

**Round 3: Pipeline and island allocation (45 minutes)**

1. Participants score each priority investment on readiness (1–5) and impact (1–5) on Sheet C. The secretariat plots the results on a readiness × impact grid during the plenary.
2. Score P9.
3. Plenary, 20 minutes: "Should a fixed minimum share of BIRD facilitation and project-preparation resources go to the island provinces?" Record yes or no with reasons; do not set a number on the day.

**Synthesis and read-back (30 minutes)**

1. The lead facilitator reads each proposition's result against the decision rules: validated, validated with amendment, or contested.
2. Contested items are read with the main reason given, not debated again.
3. MTIT-BoI states the next steps: draft results to participants within 14 days, then integration into the recalibration.

## Data handling, risks and integration

**Data handling (Data Privacy Act of 2012)**

- Read the consent briefing at registration: purpose, what is recorded, that results are reported in aggregate, and who holds the data (MTIT-BoI).
- Keep the sign-in sheet separate from the scoring sheets. Link them only by participant number.
- Encode paper sheets within 48 hours and merge them with the Mentimeter export under the same tags.

**Risks and mitigations**

| Risk | Effect on results | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small sample (20–30) | One person moves a result by 3–5 points | Report counts beside percentages; treat as signals |
| Bongao-heavy attendance | Outer islands under-represented | Record municipality; flag any proposition where outer-island views differ |
| Dominant voices or buyer–supplier power | Agreement overstated | Silent scoring first; separate farmers from buyers at tables |
| Weak connectivity | Lost digital responses | Paper backup for every round |
| Language | Propositions misread | Read each in English and the local language; facilitators check understanding |
| No farmer or cooperative voice if only firms attend | P1 and P9 skewed | Confirm at least two seaweed cooperative or association members |
| Contested macro data (GDP series) | Credibility of the briefing | Use only the Briefing facts table; hand out the errata |

**After the workshop**

- [ ] Encode and merge results; produce the validation matrix (within 7 days)
- [ ] Send draft results to participants for correction (within 14 days)
- [ ] Update Chapters 1, 3 and 7: replace "secondary data" caveats for Tawi-Tawi with workshop evidence
- [ ] Revise the Tawi-Tawi outlook and the Phase 3 evidence base
- [ ] Add Tawi-Tawi as a quota province in the 2027 second validation wave (Chapter 7, D2)
